For this assignment, I created a binary tree to organize employees based on who they report to. Recursive insertion works by starting at the team lead and checking whether the current employee matches the manager's name. If the names match the program checks whether the left or right position is available before adding the new employee. If the names don't match, the method continues searching through the branches until it finds the manager or determines that the manager doesn't exist.

One challenge I ran into was getting the recursive code in the correct place. I initially put part of it outside the insert method which made me realize how much indentation and code placement matter in Python. Another challenge was understanding how the program would find an employee who wasn't directly under the team lead. Adding Riley and Taylor under Jordan was simple, but adding Morgan and Dana under Riley helped me see why recursion was necessary. I also tested what happened when a position was already occupied or when someone entered a manager who wasn't in the directory.

Trees make more sense than lists when the relationships between information matter just as much as the information itself. A company directory is one example because employees can have managers and direct reports. File systems are another example because folders can contain other folders and files. Instead of showing everything as one long list a tree makes those connections easier to follow.
